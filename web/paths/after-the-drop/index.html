No. 13 · Your people

The other twenty hours.

For the ones who found their people at 3 a.m. and lost Wednesday.

The night is good, and you’re there for more than the night. You know the build in the chest: the wristband, the group chat since January, the stranger who grabbed your hand in the surge, the sunrise set you’ll describe for years. And the Tuesday after, lights on. Nobody is telling you to stop going. This is about the other twenty hours, and keeping what the floor gave you.

The feeling at the build is real. It can outlast Sunday.

From someone who plans the year around three dates

Three dates on my wall. EDC in May, Tomorrowland in July, and a smaller one in the desert in October. Everything else is the space between. I count down in the group chat, on a widget, in the shower. I know exactly what I’m counting toward: the mainstage at eleven, Anyma’s visuals two storeys tall, the build climbing until my ribs hum, forty thousand people holding one breath, then the drop, and the lasers open like a ceiling coming off. For four seconds I’m not a man with a lease and a manager. The nine weeks before are better than the fourth day after. The countdown is the real thing. I’m counting toward the version of me who isn’t waiting for anything. I’d like to meet him on a Wednesday. I’ve started with a Wednesday climbing wall.

From someone who stopped filming it

Fred again.. last summer, the set where he plays the voice notes over the piano and forty thousand people go quiet. I watched most of it through a phone above my head. I have eleven clips. What I noticed on the train home was that I could tell you the framing of the shot and not what it felt like when the piano came in. I was there for the story and missed the set. Next one, a Bicep show in a tent, phone zipped in the bag at the first note. I have no clips. I can tell you the exact moment the whole tent started singing the vocal back at them, and who I turned to, and that my mate was already looking round to see if I’d heard it too. That’s the one I remember.

From someone who met their people at the rail

First festival, on my own. Everyone bailed, ticket already bought, so I drove six hours and put my tent between two groups who weren’t going to talk to me. Day two, Charlotte de Witte, second row, spot held for three hours with a warm water bottle. The girl next to me had kandi up to both elbows. She looked at my bare wrists, took one off and did the thing with the hands, peace, love, unity, respect, and slid it on. Her boyfriend gave me his last electrolyte packet. When the set ended and the crowd surged we grabbed hands so nobody got lost. Seven years ago. She was at my dad’s funeral in March. He was there too, and he brought the food. I do that for one person at every set now. It’s the only ritual I keep.

From someone whose Sunday was the problem

Every set went the same. Best night of my life, then Sunday, four in the afternoon, on the couch with the group chat gone quiet and a fridge with one yoghurt in it. I’d found my people on the floor and mislaid them by Monday. What I finally noticed was that I only ever texted the rail lot about the next one. Lineups, links, tickets. Never a question. So on a Wednesday, no event within a month, I sent two of them: pub, Thursday? One came. We talked about his job for an hour, which I hadn’t known he had. That was March. It’s a standing Thursday now, four of us, no lineup, a quiz we’re bad at. The floor still gives me the best four hours of the month. Thursday is where the rest of it lives.

From someone on the Tuesday after

Tuesday. The wristband’s still on. Three days ago I was at a Rüfüs sunrise set with my arms round two people I’d met the night before, the sky went pink behind the stage, and I cried, and nobody thought it was odd. Today I had cereal for dinner and couldn’t answer a text. I’m not going to pretend the bill is only lost sleep. I drank more than I meant to, and that comes due on about day three, and today is day three. Grey and flat. What helps a bit: I know it passes. What helps more: my rail friend messaged this morning, not about the next one, just, how are you doing, drink water. Sleeping tonight instead of the aftermovie. Wristband comes off Thursday. Decided that on purpose.

From someone steady, still going at thirty-four

Thirty-four, still going. Fewer, and differently. I used to do four days on a campsite; now it’s one night, a Berlin basement that runs from midnight to six, when I walk out with a coffee from the bakery that opens for exactly us. Fisher at the club, hands in the air, the whole room grinning like idiots. What changed isn’t that I love it less. I stopped asking it to do a job it can’t do. In my twenties every set was an audition. Right group, right person watching. I was checking, and you can’t dance while you’re checking. Now I go with the same three people. We leave when someone’s done. The drop still hits. When I get home, someone’s there who knows what the night was for and never wanted to be on the guest list.

What actually works

None of these ask you to stop going. All of them are about keeping what the night gives you. Pick one this week.

  1. One set, all the way through, alone, lights off.The one you’d put in your obituary. No phone, no aftermovie, start to finish. Notice what happens in your chest at the build. That’s what you’re there for.
  2. Text the rail friend on a Wednesday.Not about the next one. No lineup, no link. Ask how they are, and see what comes back when there’s nothing to plan.
  3. A sober set, once.Just to see if the feeling’s still there. It is. And now you’ll know it was yours.
  4. Sleep the full night after, before the group chat.The aftermovie will keep. The chat will still be there at noon. Give your body the night back before you hand the weekend to your phone.
  5. Put one thing in the diary that has nothing to do with a lineup.A pub quiz, a kickabout, a Thursday curry with two people from the rail. The crew that turns up when there’s nothing on is the crew.

The code

  • The feeling at the build is real. Keep it.
  • The night is four hours. Build the other twenty.
  • The crew that texts on a Wednesday is the crew.
  • Water, sleep, and the aftermovie keeps.
  • Nobody at the rail is keeping score.
  • The best night of your life shouldn’t be the only one.
  • Joy came before the floor. You were made on purpose.

In fairness to the night

What the night gets right.

Nobody is asking you to stop. The floor teaches things a quiet room can’t. Keep these.

  • Strangers can be kind for no reason. Nobody is paying them to.
  • A crowd can move as one and nobody has to be in charge of it.
  • You already know how to feel joy. Nobody had to teach you.
  • The night goes better when nobody is checking who’s watching.
  • The sunrise set is better with someone who’ll remember it with you.
  • The hand that grabbed yours in the surge meant it.
  • The quiet after the last set isn’t nothing. It’s where the other twenty hours live.
