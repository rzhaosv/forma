A path · Being seen

For the one who is more than the pretty one.

You are more than the one who holds the bags, and someone is going to know it. This is for the friend at the edge of the frame, the funny one, the one who learned early to be useful instead of seen. No advice here about your face, your body, or fixing anything, because nothing about you needs fixing. Just the difference between being noticed and being known, and why you are owed the second.

Being known is coming. You are owed it.

From her

I am in every photo from that trip. You just cannot see me, because I am the one taking it, or the one holding four bags and a jacket at the edge of the frame. Nobody asked me to stand there. I just knew where to go. I learned early that if I could not be the one they looked at, I could be the one who made sure everything worked. Last month a friend took the camera out of my hands and said, get in. I am in the middle of that one. I look like someone who belongs there.

From her

The worst part is not being called ugly. Nobody does that. The worst part is standing next to her when someone says how pretty she is, and the sentence has room for exactly one person. It is not said against me. It still lands on me, like a verdict read out in a room where I happen to be standing. Except it is not a verdict. It is one sentence about one thing, from someone looking fast. The people who look slowly say different things, and I am starting to listen to them instead.

From someone who sees her

There is a difference between being noticed and being known, and you have been on the wrong side of the first for so long that you have stopped believing in the second. Noticed is a glance across a room. Known is someone remembering that you hate crowded restaurants and booking the corner table. You have been doing the second for everyone else for years. You are owed it back.

From someone who sees her

Some beauty shows up in a photo. Some shows up in how a person is looked after: who noticed you were quiet, who saved you a seat, who texted after the party to ask if you got home. That is a beauty people do not know how to compliment, so they do not. It is still the one they miss most when it is gone.

From someone who sees her

The loud, easy-to-look-at ones get the attention at twenty. Watch what happens after. The person everyone ends up coming home to, calling at 2am, trusting with the hard news, is very often the quiet one at the edge of the frame who never once made it about herself. The last one anyone looked at becomes the first one anyone needs.

From someone who sees her

You were made carefully, on purpose, and nothing about you was an accident. Not your face, not your laugh, not the way you notice everything. Somebody who knew what they were looking for would give up a great deal for exactly what you are, and would not ask you to change a single thing first. Being overlooked is not evidence. It is just what happens when people look fast.

Things that are true today

Not steps to take. Facts to notice. Write them down if you can.

  1. One thing you did well this week.Name it specifically. Small counts.
  2. One person who is glad you exist.Say their name out loud. You know who it is.
  3. One compliment you will give yourself, in writing.Not about your face. About something you are.
  4. One photo where you are in the middle.Ask someone to take it. Stand in the middle on purpose, and keep it.
  5. One friend you will tell the truth to.About how it feels to be the frame. Let them know you.
