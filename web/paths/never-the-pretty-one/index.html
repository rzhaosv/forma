No. 05 · Coming into your own

Second from the left.

For the friend who held the coats, and what she is owed.

Someone decided early who the pretty one was, and it wasn't you. So you learned where to stand: the edge of the picture, the coats over one arm, the one who booked the table and made sure everyone got home. This is for her. Not a word about your face. Just being noticed, on purpose, and being known, which is the better of the two.

Someone kept the light on. Being known is coming, and you are owed it.

Noted, from her

Second from the left, every photo, eleven years running. Or not in it at all, because I was the one taking it. Nobody put me there. I just knew where to go. Last spring a friend took the phone out of my hand at her sister's wedding and said, get in. I stood in the middle, which felt like standing on a stage. I kept that one. It is on the fridge now, next to the dentist card. I look like someone who belongs in the picture, because I do. Next time, I'm not waiting to be asked. I'll hand the phone to someone and walk into the middle myself.

Noted, from her

Standing next to her when someone says how lovely she looks. The sentence has room for exactly one person, and it isn't said against me. It just lands where I'm standing. I used to take it home and turn it over for a week. What I noticed, eventually, is who says it: people looking fast, across a room, on the way somewhere. The people who look slowly say different things. That I'm the one who remembers birthdays. That the evening only worked because I was there. I've started listening to the slow ones instead. Quick glances don't get a vote any more. It's a small change of who I let count, and it changed a lot.

From someone who noticed her

There's a difference between being noticed and being known. Noticed is a glance across a room. Known is someone remembering you hate crowded restaurants and booking the corner table without being asked. You've been doing the second for everyone else for years: the spare charger in your bag, the text after the party to check they got home. Here is what I'd do with that. Keep doing it, and start letting it come back. Say yes when someone offers to pick you up. Tell one friend which restaurant you actually like. People can't look after someone who never lets them. You've had the harder skill the whole time; being noticed is mostly a matter of standing where the light is.

Noted, from her

I made an effort for myself in March. Nothing dramatic: a proper haircut, the one dress I actually like instead of the one that disappears, and I stopped standing by the door at parties. I picked a spot near the kitchen where people come through, and I stayed there. Three people I'd known for years started conversations with me that night, and one of them said, I don't think we've ever really talked. We had. She'd just never been looking. That's not a complaint. It's a note about where I'd been standing. I still hold coats sometimes, because I like being useful. I just put them down when the music starts now.

From someone who noticed her

Watch what happens after twenty-five. The ones everyone looked at first get looked at first; that's all it was, an order of looking. The one people ring at two in the morning, trust with the hard news, come home to, is very often the one who never made it about herself. My sister is that one. Every family has her, every office, every group of friends. She isn't a consolation prize. She's the centre of the whole thing, and the people who know that would give up a great deal for her. So the useful thing isn't to become the first one looked at. It's to stop apologising for being the one everyone actually needs, and to let one or two of them say so.

The steady one

Here is where I've landed, after a lot of years at the edge of the picture. Being overlooked isn't evidence of anything. It's what happens when people look fast, and most people look fast. I was made on purpose, before anyone had an opinion about my face, by someone who wasn't grading. That didn't arrive as a feeling. It arrived as a decision I make most mornings, in the mirror, before the day gets a say. Then I go and do the ordinary things: the haircut, the good coat, standing in the middle of the photo, letting a friend book the table for me for once. Given, not earned. I still notice who's looking. I just stopped waiting for permission to be seen.

What actually works

Nothing clever. Pick one this week.

  1. Stand in the middle of one photo.Hand the phone to someone else. Keep the picture where you'll see it.
  2. Let someone do one thing for you.The lift, the table, the coats. Say yes and don't apologise for it.
  3. Make an effort for yourself, once a week.The haircut, the dress you actually like. Not for a room; for the mirror on a Tuesday.
  4. Tell one friend the true version.One sentence about what it's like at the edge of the picture. Let her know you.
  5. Write yourself the compliment you'd give a friend.Not about your face. About something you are. Keep it in the notes app.

The code

  • Being overlooked is not evidence.
  • Quick glances don't get a vote.
  • Known beats noticed. Have both anyway.
  • Put the coats down when the music starts.
  • Stand where the light is.
  • Let people look after you back.
  • You were made on purpose. Walk into the middle of the photo.
