No. 07 · Coming into your own

Looking after yourself.

Posture, sleep, a good haircut, and the one measurement nobody at the gym takes.

You've looked after yourself. Posture, sleep, a proper haircut, the side of your face the camera prefers. You did the reading and you did the practice, and none of that was wasted. This is for the part after the mirror: the question under all the measurements, whether you were ever going to be the one. Wanting to be noticed is not the problem. Here is where to point it.

You were made on purpose. Composure first; the rest follows.

Noted, his

I know my face better than anyone I love. Which angle, which light, which app is kind and which one isn't. Noted. Then I noticed something else: every scan was supposed to settle it, and every scan started the count again. So I ran an experiment. Deleted one rating app on a Sunday night, kept the gym, kept the skincare, kept the sleep. First thing that happened was I slept eight hours, which I hadn't done since March. Second thing, about a week in, was catching myself in a shop window and not checking. Just walking past. The apps measured a face. They never once measured whether I was easy to be around. Getting the hours in on that one now.

From someone steady

The comparison never closes. There's always a taller lad, a leaner one, one with better numbers, and the moment you pass one of them the ranking refreshes and puts another in front. That's not a flaw in the game; that's the game. Here's what I do instead, and I lift four days a week so this isn't a speech against the gym. I keep the reps, and I stopped keeping score. Nobody at my table is being measured. When a mate walks in looking well I say so, out loud, and it costs me nothing, because his good week isn't taken out of mine. There's more than one of everything. That took me longer to believe than any lift.

From someone steady

A rating from a stranger is a number that evaporates. It feels like proof for about ninety seconds and then you need another one. Meanwhile the people who actually know you aren't rating you at all. They're remembering that you helped them move on a Saturday, that you rang when it mattered. Being looked at gets you a number. Being known gets you a place. Only one of those lasts, and it's the one you can't screenshot. So here's the practical version. Once a week, do one thing for someone that can't be posted. Carry the thing, show up early, drive them to the airport. Presence is earned in rooms, not mirrors. You'll be surprised who starts looking at you differently.

From someone steady

Train hard. Eat well. Sleep. Keep all of it; the boring stuff works. Just check who it's serving. Showing up whether you feel like it or not, when it serves your life, makes you stronger for the people who need you and the thing you're building. The same routine serving a scoreboard makes you afraid of a missed day, a bad photo, the mirror at the wrong angle. Same workout, different boss. The test I use: after the session, do I feel like someone with a bit about him, or someone waiting to be marked? If it's the second, I skip the mirror photo and go and do something with my hands. The body is a tool. It was never meant to be the whole house.

Noted, his

Around February I put the phone down. Deleted two apps, kept the gym, kept the haircut appointment. Started building a small furniture thing with a lad from work, badly at first; the first table wobbled and we sold it anyway, cheap, to his cousin. Nobody rated it. Nobody's rated anything I've done since, and I've done more in six months than in the two years before. The strange part is that people say I look well now, and I couldn't tell you what changed. I think it's that I stopped checking. My face got to be a face again instead of a case file. Next is a proper workshop, a lock-up behind the garage, and a second table that doesn't wobble.

The steady one

I look after myself. Posture, sleep, a proper haircut, the side of my face the camera prefers; I know which one. I did the reading and I did the practice, and I'll keep doing it, because looking after yourself is a kind of respect. What I didn't have a measurement for was the question under the measurements: whether I was ever going to be the one. Here's where I've landed. Composure first. Presence is earned in rooms, not mirrors. The face is the house; it isn't the one who lives there. And the strangest, steadiest thing: I was made on purpose, before anyone had an opinion, by someone who wasn't grading. Given, not earned. I still train. I just stopped auditioning.

What actually works

Keep the gym. Add one of these this week.

  1. One session with no mirror photo after.Just the work and the shower. Notice how it feels not to file it.
  2. Delete one rating app, don't just close it.Give it a week. Watch what you do with the freed-up minutes.
  3. Tell another man he's looking well.Out loud, no joke attached. His good week isn't taken out of yours.
  4. Twenty minutes a day on something that will never be posted.A skill, a repair, a language. Presence is built in rooms nobody's filming.
  5. Be somewhere on time for someone.Pick them. Turn up. Do the thing you said you'd do.

The code

  • Looking after yourself is respect, not rescue.
  • Composure before the mirror.
  • Presence is earned in rooms, not mirrors.
  • No opinion you didn't ask for gets a vote.
  • The boring stuff works: sleep, water, sun, practice.
  • Nobody at this table is being measured.
  • You were made on purpose. Act like someone who was.
