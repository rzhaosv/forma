Out there

Playing it long.

For the founder checking someone else's numbers when they should be asleep.

Wanting to build something real is a good hunger, and it's yours whether or not anyone screenshots it. This is for the ones refreshing strangers' dashboards at 2 a.m.: the MRR posts, the funding threads, the lists, and measuring a whole life against them. That scoreboard is curated, and it was never where the thing you're after lives. The nine customers in front of you are.

Nine customers, looked after, is a real company.

2 a.m., someone else's dashboard

2 a.m., a stranger's dashboard screenshot. Forty-one thousand MRR, up and to the right, posted with a shrug emoji. Three things I know about him: his first name, his number, and that he's ahead of me. Whether he sleeps, no idea. My own product has nine paying customers and I hadn't opened it all day, because I'd been busy measuring. Noted. Closed the tab, opened the product. One of the nine had written in to say thanks for a feature nobody else has asked for. That's the number I'm looking at tomorrow, and the tab stays closed until nine.

The scoreboard is curated

The scoreboard is curated. Nobody posts the churn month, the co-founder who left, the round that fell through, the Tuesday in the car park. You're comparing your full ledger, losses included, against everyone else's highlight reel, and that was never a fair fight. A raise is a bet someone placed, not a result anyone achieved; the result comes years later and is mostly quiet. What I did about it: I wrote my own ledger, on paper, once a week. Customers helped, things shipped, hours slept, one honest number. Twelve weeks in, the pile of paper says more about the company than the feed ever did, and I sleep before midnight most nights.

Building it, and being seen to build it

There's building something, and there's being seen to build something, and the feed pays out for the second so reliably people forget the first exists. I watched it happen on my own team. The one with the smaller number picked up the phone when a customer's export broke at midnight and sorted it, because a real person needed it. The one with the bigger number was writing a thread about the export. Both looked fine on the timeline. Only one of them still has that customer. So I made a rule: nothing gets posted about until it's been used by someone who paid for it. The posting got rarer and the product got better. On the day the internet stops looking, I know which one I'd rather be.

What holds when the weather turns

What does it get you, winning the whole thing and losing yourself on the way? A bigger number and nobody home to enjoy it. I've seen two companies with the same headline figure go into the same bad quarter. One had a founder who'd slept, a team who'd been told the truth, and customers who'd been called back. The other had a great thread. The feed is sunshine; everything looks sound in sunshine. What told them apart was the weather. So now I build for the weather: the honest update, the boring week of shipping, the customer rung back the same day. None of it screenshots well. All of it was still standing in March, and one of those two companies wasn't.

Nine became fourteen

Muted the founder accounts in March and spent the hour I used to spend refreshing on one customer at a time instead. An email, a call, a bug closed with their name on it. Nine became fourteen by June. Not a story anyone would post, and I didn't. What changed wasn't the number; it was that I stopped feeling like I was pretending. I was finally doing the thing rather than watching people announce it. Nobody clapped when the thing got planted. The hour's still blocked out, same time every morning, feed closed, and I've not been back to check what forty-one thousand is up to.

A quiet word from someone further along

The one who could be trusted with a little got handed more. Not the one with the biggest launch. The one who could be trusted with nine customers, an honest update, a boring week of shipping. I've watched it play out over fifteen years and it's held every time, just slowly, which is why nobody posts about it. You weren't put here to be a line on somebody else's leaderboard. You were put here to look after the people in front of you with what you've got. Do that well, play it long, and whatever the figure turns out to be, it'll be yours. Then close the laptop. It's late.

What actually works

None of these show up on a leaderboard. All of them compound. Pick one this week.

  1. One customer you personally helped this week.By name. Not a ticket closed. A person whose day got better because you exist.
  2. One hour of proper work with the feed closed.Phone in another room. One problem. See how far you get when nobody else's number is in the room.
  3. One honest update to someone you owe one.An investor, a co-founder, a customer, your partner. The real number, the real timeline. Relief follows.
  4. One set time a day to check the dashboard.Once, at nine, never in bed. It will still be there.
  5. One thing you shipped that you'd stand behind.Small is fine. Finished and useful is the bar. Write it down where the screenshots used to go.

The code

  • Their screenshot is not your scoreboard.
  • Nobody posts the churn month.
  • A raise is a bet placed, not a result achieved.
  • Build the part that holds when the weather turns.
  • Being seen to build it is not building it.
  • Check the numbers once a day, and never in bed.
  • Nine customers were given, not earned. Look after them.
