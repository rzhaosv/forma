The sequence

Exactly what you do, in order

This is the sequence as it actually runs in 2026, with what each step costs and how long it waits. Read the first step twice. The compounded lane he used is the one lane that is closing.

  1. Pick the lane before you pick the drug.The semaglutide shortage ended in February 2025, so mass compounding is over. In 2026 a compounded GLP-1 is legal only as a patient-specific 503A prescription with a clinical reason, and the FDA has sent more than fifty warning letters this year; Hims and Ro have left compounded semaglutide entirely. The lanes still open: brand-name GLP-1 at the manufacturer’s cash price with your telehealth fee on top, the 503A lane with a real clinical reason and a lawyer, or the same machine pointed at testosterone, hair, ED, menopause or longevity, all of which the infrastructure partner already stocks. MedVi itself runs a men’s health line for exactly this reason.
  2. Start with OpenLoop.OpenLoop Health is the clinical layer MedVi rents: licensed clinicians in all fifty states, the prescriptions, the pharmacy fulfilment, and the compliant intake flow. You need no medical licence and no doctor on payroll. The deal is custom-quoted, so book the sales call first, because it is the longest lead time you control. You sign a service agreement with a price schedule; you set your retail above their floor on each plan and keep the difference. On a monthly plan expect to keep roughly a third of a $169 price, and far more on prepaid quarters and years, which is why every ad you see pushes the long plan. They give you a hosted intake page and a client dashboard where you edit prices per plan. Two rules from inside: their medical steps cannot be deleted from the intake, only their promotion blocks can be edited, and their support form answers by the next business day, so send the pushback there rather than waiting.
  3. Build the site with AI, in a day.MedVi’s site is not a website in the old sense. It is a quiz funnel: a landing page, three questions, a fit verdict, a price, then a handoff to the partner’s intake with the plan in the URL. Hand that spec to Claude Code or ChatGPT and it scaffolds the whole thing in an afternoon: landing, quiz, result page, pricing, privacy policy, terms, a prescription policy page, and the pixel. Keep the quiz to three questions. The buyer already knows what a GLP-1 is; the quiz exists to qualify, not to educate. Route the last click straight to the intake. Do not build your own checkout; the partner’s intake is the checkout, and it is the part that already passed compliance.
  4. Write no claim you cannot defend, then freeze the copy.The words that got MedVi its warning letter were on the website, not in the ads: language that implied FDA approval and implied the company was the compounder. Before certification, read every sentence on the site as a regulator would. No “FDA-approved”, no “compounded” in the marketing, no outcomes promised, no before-and-after pictures, and no testimonials until you can prove them. Then stop touching the words. Certification is a review of a snapshot, and every edit during review is a reason to start the clock again.
  5. Apply for LegitScript Healthcare Merchant Certification.This is the gate everything else waits on. Meta, Google, TikTok and the card networks will not run a telehealth prescription business without it. The 2026 fee is $975 to apply plus $2,150 a year per website, and $2,500 more for expedited processing, which is worth paying. They verify the provider licences, the pharmacy, your prescribing practice, your privacy policy and every marketing claim. Standard processing runs four to eight weeks; expedited moved a clean application from filing to analyst review in three days and then sat in review for two weeks. Have the entity, the domain, the business email, the partner agreement and the policy pages ready before you file, because the questions come in rounds and each round you are slow on is a week.
  6. Meta, in this order.Business verification in Business Manager first, then domain verification of your site, then a payment method, then two weeks of ordinary use of the account with no ads running. Day-one ads on a day-one account is the most common reason a health advertiser gets banned before they spend a dollar. Once the LegitScript certificate is active, apply for Meta’s written permission to advertise prescription drugs; it is a form, it takes days, and the permission is limited to the United States, Canada and New Zealand, adults only. Accept the Custom Audience terms while you are there, or the retargeting audiences will fail later and you will not know why.
  7. Then the ads, the way he ran them.One flat price in the headline. “Prescription GLP-1 care” in the copy, never the word compounded. A real person’s face, with a signed release, not a persona. Twenty variants across three formats, a $50 a day budget, one metric, which is cost per qualified lead. Kill anything past eight dollars of spend with nothing to show. When one wins, leave it alone for a quarter. Then, and only then, add affiliates on their own pages, paid per patient delivered.
  8. Budget and calendar, honestly.Week zero: entity, bank account, domain, business email. Week one: OpenLoop call and contract, site built. Week two: LegitScript filed, expedited. Weeks three to six: Meta verification and account warm-up while certification runs. Weeks six to eight: permission granted, first ads live. Cash to the first paying patient is under ten thousand dollars, and most of it is the certification and the first month of ads. The thing you cannot buy is the eight weeks, so start the two slow clocks, OpenLoop and LegitScript, in the same week.

Eight weeks and under ten thousand dollars gets you a legal version of the machine. What it will not get you is his window, because that one has closed. Pick a lane that is open.
