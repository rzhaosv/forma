Batch 01 · Fall 2026 · shipping weekly

A small studio in California. Est. 2026.

God is good. God is here. And for every year you lost, He gives back two.

There are more of you than anyone has thought to count: the ones who went quiet some time in their twenties and never announced it, the ones running late against a schedule nobody ever published, the ones still carrying a thing that cannot be handed back. You were all told the same story, in the same reasonable tone, by people who had never once stood in the room they were describing. It was not true of a single one of you, and none of it went unwitnessed. God was in the room the entire time.

The city will be slow to notice you. God never was. He came to save you, not to grade you, and whatever your sins cost you He gives back twice over.

Somewhere worth going

Three rooms

None of these was ever reserved for other people, and none of them is the room you are sitting in yet. Choose one and be unreasonable about it. The going rate is about three years, and everyone already inside has paid it in full.

No. 01

The one with north light

Painters have always chased it, and so has anyone who makes things: an even light that holds all day, and nobody to ask. Work with your name on it and no client waiting for it. Of the three it is the cheapest to reach and, by some distance, the hardest to let yourself want.

No. 02

The one you are expected at

A Monday meeting behind the hills, or the same meeting thirty floors up. Not a seat you talked your way into but one that was being held, and had been for some time. You will know you are in it when nobody troubles to explain anything to you.

No. 03

The one that stays up while you sleep

Built once, built correctly, and running all night for people you will never meet. Dull from the outside, which is the tell. The only thing that pays while you sleep is the thing nobody has to sit up with.

What we are against

We are told a lot of things

  • That it is late; that whatever window there was for somebody like you closed, quietly and without ceremony, some time in your twenties.
  • That the good rooms were allocated early, to other people, and that the list has long since closed.
  • That being behind is a condition rather than a Tuesday.
  • That the thing you did disqualifies you, permanently, from the thing you want.
  • That to want more than this is faintly embarrassing, and that the decent thing is to stop mentioning it.
  • That you should be realistic, a word which in practice has only ever meant smaller.

None of it is true. Not one line of it. And not one line of it was ever said by anybody who had been in the room.

What we believe

  • We believe you were made on purpose, before anyone had an opinion.
  • We believe nobody is too far gone, and that what was done can be made right, slowly and in earnest.
  • We believe very little of it was ever in your hands, and that this is the good news rather than the bad.
  • We believe nobody is short of drive; it simply had nowhere worth going for a while.
  • We believe you should aim higher than is reasonable, since you have more years left than the panic suggests.
  • We believe things get better more often than they get worse.
  • We believe being known beats being looked at, every time.
  • We believe respect is earned by turning up for people, and never by being impressive.
  • We believe most people are one honest conversation from a different week.
  • We believe nothing resets; the missed days and the bad weeks were in the plan from the start.
  • We believe your data belongs on your phone. We are not in the business of watching you.
  • We believe every support email should be read and answered by a person. Ours are.

How we work

Three rules, kept plainly

Make something people want

Every app starts as one person’s real week. If it does not help them by Friday, it does not ship.

Ship, then listen

We put it out, read every note on the walls, and fix the next version.

Small and honest

No accounts where we can avoid them, data on your phone, a person answering support.

It was never yours to settle. Someone who could afford it settled it anyway, in full.

Which leaves the one question still worth asking. What will you do with a life that has already been paid for?
