Population 8,200,000,000. Group chat, 8.

See how small you are. It is the best news you will get today.

Every timeline you have ever measured yourself against was drawn by the same eight women, and every one of them lives within forty miles of you. Below is the rest of it: the other 8,199,999,992, none of whom have seen the ring in the caption, most of whom are having a perfectly good Tuesday without knowing your age. Drag it. Let it turn. Find yourself, and then notice how quickly the camera leaves you behind.

There is a dot the size of one pixel on the east side of the bay. That is you. It has a ring around it so you can find it; nothing else on this ball has ever needed one. Tap a pin, or pick a room below and the globe will take you there.

Company, by the hour

The above-ground version. Tokyo worked out years ago that company is a service like any other, Shanghai turned it into a New Year tradition, and the rest of the world is catching up on TikTok. None of these is a host club. All of them are the kind of thing you would send the group chat, and should.

Tokyo · date only

The rental boyfriend

A licensed, above-board service: he meets you at the station, carries the shopping, takes the photos you actually want taken, and is interested in your day for exactly three hours. About ¥6,000 an hour. Nobody touches anybody, by contract. A city of fourteen million decided being accompanied is worth paying for, and they were right.

Shanghai · Lunar New Year

Every January, thousands of women in China rent a boyfriend on Taobao for the trip home, so the questions at the table stop for one year. It is a joke, an industry and a kindness at the same time, and it went viral for a reason: everyone recognised the table.

      Room three

      Run the thing you already run for free

      Nobody is going to tell you what women like. You already run the dinner series, the run club, the book club, the group trip spreadsheet, the ceramics table on Thursdays. You have been the operator for years; the only thing missing is the invoice. Here is the ledger in the order it actually happens, and then the eight places it actually stalls, none of which is the LLC.

      1. Name the thing you already run.The supper club, the Saturday run, the matcha table, the newsletter you send to forty people. It has been a business for a year; it just has not been told.
      2. Make it a company.A California or Wyoming LLC through Northwest Registered Agent or Stripe Atlas. About three hundred dollars and forty minutes. You are now a founder in the eyes of the state, which is the only set of eyes that matters at this step.
      3. Open the account.Mercury, from your kitchen, in a day or two. A card with the company's name on it. From here on the flowers, the ingredients and the studio hour do not come out of your own pocket, which is the first time it feels real.
      4. Give it a door.Luma or Partiful for the event, one page on Vercel with one sentence and one way to pay, Stripe or Square for the money.
      5. Charge the first friend.The hardest step on this list. Say the number without the laugh after it.
      6. Find the ninth regular.Nine people who come back, by name, is a real company. It was always going to be. Nobody screenshots it, and that is fine.

      Where it actually stalls

      1. Charging friends.Say a number, then stop talking. The awkwardness lasts four seconds and then it is a business. Friends who would rather it were free are not customers, and that is fine; they were guests, and guests are also allowed.
      2. Food, and the law.California's cottage food permit covers baked goods, jams and the like from your own kitchen for a few hundred dollars a year. Anything cooked and served needs a commissary kitchen by the hour or a caterer's permit. Dinners for friends in your home are one thing; ticketed dinners for strangers are the other thing. Know which one you are running before the county asks.
      3. The lease math.A studio, a reformer room, a bar: price per seat per hour, times the seats you can fill on a Tuesday, not a Saturday. If the Tuesday number does not cover the rent, it is a Saturday business, and Saturday businesses are real too; they just should not sign a five-year lease.
      4. Instagram to payment.Four thousand followers converts to about forty. Put the link where the thumb lands, make paying take one screen, and stop apologising in the caption.
      5. Who am I to.Nobody who runs a supper club is a chef. Nobody who runs a run club is fast. The qualification is that you kept showing up and other people came, and you already have it.
      6. Week three.The second dinner sells out and the third does not, and you conclude it was a phase. It was not. Week three is where every one-person company is decided, and the decision is to send one more invitation.
      7. The tax you did not set aside.Move thirty percent of every payment into a second account the day it lands. Estimates are due in April, June, September and January. Nobody warned you because nobody remembers being warned.
      8. Telling the group chat.Tell them. Then charge them.
