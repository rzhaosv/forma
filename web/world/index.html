Somewhere bigger

Population 8,200,000,000. Group chat, 8.

See how small you are. It is the best news you will get today.

Every scoreboard you have ever lost on had the same eight names at the top, and every one of them lives within forty miles of you. Below is the rest of it: the other 8,199,999,992, none of whom have heard of the group chat, most of whom are having a perfectly good Tuesday without your title. Drag it. Let it turn. Find yourself, and then notice how quickly the camera leaves you behind.

Where you are

Somewhere down there, near the fog

There is a dot the size of one pixel on the peninsula. That is you. It has a ring around it so you can find it; nothing else on this ball has ever needed one. Tap any of the others.

You have not lain awake wondering whether Wonyoung thinks you are behind. For years, in private, you have been practising the one skill this whole site is trying to teach: loving something on purpose, at full volume, with nothing in it for you. That was never the guilty pleasure. That was the muscle.

Room two

Make a call

The itch is real: to have a view about how the world will go, and to be right about it. Every app that sells you that charges for it, and the fee was never the money. Here it costs nothing, nobody sees your record, and being wrong is a Tuesday. Make your calls. Come back Sunday and mark them. Keep your own score, or do not.

    Stored on this device only. No money changes hands, and no one but you will ever see the record. That is the entire point of it.

    Room three

    Start something the size of one person

    Not the company that gets reported. A company. One person, one thing people pay for, one bank account with a name on it that is not your employer's. It is more boring than the group chat makes it sound, and the boring part is the whole of it. Here is the ledger, in the order it actually happens.

    1. Name it.Two words, spelled the way they sound. Check the domain. Do not spend a weekend on this; the name is the least of it.
    2. Make it a company.A Delaware or Wyoming LLC through Northwest Registered Agent or Stripe Atlas. About three hundred dollars and forty minutes. You are now a founder in the eyes of the state, which is the only set of eyes that matters at this step.
    3. Open the account.Mercury, from your kitchen, in a day. A card with the company's name on it. From here on nothing for the company comes out of your own pocket, which is the first time it feels real.
    4. Put up a page.One page, one sentence about what it does, one way to pay. Vercel is free until it is not, and by then you will not mind.
    5. Charge the first person.Stripe. The first payment is small, and it changes something in you that no promotion ever has.
    6. Find the ninth.Nine customers, looked after by name, is a real company. It was always going to be. Nobody screenshots it, and that is fine.
