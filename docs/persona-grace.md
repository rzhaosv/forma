# The second reader: Grace

The women's side of tryforma.app is written for one person, the way the men's side is written for Kevin. Same trap, different scoreboard.

## Grace, 32, Oakland

Grew up in Cupertino or Irvine, or came to Cal at eighteen and stayed. Parents who said "we just want you to be happy" and meant "we want you to be safe." Piano until sixteen, debate, the good school, a degree that photographs well. Now a product manager, a designer, a pharmacist, a fourth-year associate, or an engineer at a company her aunties have heard of. She is good at her job and has been told so, in writing, by people whose approval she stopped needing years ago and still saves.

Rents a one-bedroom in Temescal or the Inner Richmond. A Subaru or a Mini. A Pilates membership she uses. A sourdough starter she does not.

## The trap

Her scoreboard is not titles. It is timelines.

The group chat is six women from the freshman dorm and two from work. One got engaged in Kyoto and the ring was in the caption. One had a baby and the birth announcement was designed. One went to HBS and posts from airports. One moved to New York and became, apparently, a different and more interesting person. One quit tech to open a studio and is somehow fine. Grace was at every one of those weddings, in the dress they chose, and has a folder of the photos she has not looked at.

She is not behind. Everyone tells her so. She has done the math on the timeline anyway, and the math has a date on it, and the date is not far.

Hinge is open in a tab she did not mean to open. So is Zillow. So is a cousin's Instagram.

## What she actually wants

A life she would choose again on a Tuesday, not one that photographs. To adore something loudly without it being a personality. To run the thing she already runs for free, the dinner series, the run club, the group trip spreadsheet, as a real thing with a name and a bank account. To have a view about the world and be right, for nothing. To look after her face and her frame because they are hers, not because of a date.

She was raised in a Korean or Chinese church and drifted. She would take grace if it came without a sales funnel.

## How she reads

Sharp, warm, funny in the second half of the sentence. Allergic to being managed, to "girlboss," to wellness copy, to being told what women like. Smells a stereotype in four words and closes the tab. Respects specificity: Temescal, not "the East Bay." Loves a well-made thing and notices the seams on a badly made one.

She does not want a coach or a bestie. She wants a friend a step ahead who is not counting.

## Rules, in addition to Kevin's ten

1. **No timelines.** Never a date, an age cutoff, a clock, a window. The site is the one room where nobody asks.
2. **Never tell her what women like.** Describe the thing she already does, by its real name, and offer to make it real.
3. **The reward is a jjimjilbang, not a spa day.** Specific, cheap, communal, hers.
4. **Adoration is loud and unembarrassed.** SEVENTEEN at full volume in the car is the muscle, same as it is for him.

## The four rooms on her side

- **Someone to adore.** Male idols and the K-drama leads: BTS, SEVENTEEN, Stray Kids, TXT, ENHYPEN, ATEEZ, Cha Eun-woo, Byeon Woo-seok. Plus company by the hour: Tokyo's rental boyfriends who only hold your shopping bag, the butler café, dinner with five strangers.
- **Make a call.** Same Kalshi room, her own board.
- **Run the thing you already run for free.** The supper club, the run club, the book club, the ceramics table, the matcha bar, the newsletter. The ledger is the same; the sticking points are different: food permits, the studio lease, charging friends.
- **The cut and the build.** A frame she chooses, a haircut brief for the hush cut or the curtain bangs, three skincare steps, and the jjimjilbang as the rest.
